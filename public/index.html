Cultural Collections

The University of Melbourne's cultural collections are unrivalled among Australian universities, spanning historical and contemporary collections across visual arts, cartography, medical history, zoology, archives and more, including significant collections of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander cultural heritage.

Our five museums and collections

Grainger Museum

The personal archive, instruments and scores of composer Percy Grainger, held in trust for scholarship worldwide.

Explore the Grainger Museum →

Medical History Museum

Explore, inquire and enjoy the social history of medicine and its role in society through a diverse exhibition program. Visit the nineteenth century Savory and Moore Pharmacy complete with pharmaceutical jars and equipment.

Explore the Medical History Museum →

University Art Collection

Paintings, prints, sculpture and decorative arts spanning classical antiquity to contemporary Australian practice.

Explore the University Art Collection →

Henry Forman Atkinson Dental Museum

Visit the Henry Forman Atkinson Dental Museum to be inspired by the intriguing history of dental health and the development of the dental professions and dental education. It is the oldest and most significant dental collection in Australia, tracing its origins to 1884.

Explore the Dental Museum →

Harry Brookes Allen Museum

One of Australia's largest collections of human tissue specimens, animal anatomy specimens and historical anatomical models, offering students and researchers a unique insight into the human body.

Explore the Harry Brookes Allen Museum →

How search works

A few tips to help you find what you're looking for across the collection.

The search box searches everything, so it's the best place to start. Use two or three keywords – searches often fail because they are too specific. All your keywords must appear in a record (an AND search). Suggestions appear as you type; they come from terms in the catalogue, so picking one always returns results.

Filters let you refine your results by structured data – format type, collection, date ranges and subject – and show the number of results per option. If your result set is large, review the filters to see what format, creators or subjects are represented, and select the ones most relevant to your query. Once you select a filter, it stays in effect until removed, including if you start a new search.

Use double quotation marks around your terms to match words in the exact order you typed them – for example, "architectural plan".

Want to search a single field – title, creator, subject or date range? Or combine several fields using Contains all (AND), Contains any (OR) or Does not contain (NOT)? Use advanced search when you need to combine two or more specific fields, combine two or more values of a filter, or search items across two selected collections.

Create complex logic using AND, OR and NOT (all in upper case) and parentheses for nesting.

Before you visit

Items across the collections are catalogued under three access levels, shown on every record: Open Access, By Appointment, and Restricted. Some items also carry a cultural or advisory notice for sensitive material – please engage respectfully with this content. To request access or further information about an item, contact the relevant collection and include the record's persistent link.
