Search tips

Basic search

The search box searches everything, so it's the best place to start. Use two or three keywords – searches often fail because they are too specific. All your keywords must appear in a record (an AND search). Suggestions appear as you type; they come from terms in the catalogue, so picking one always returns results.

Search in a single field

Use the dropdown beside the search box when a common word pulls in results you don't want.

Ranking

Results are ranked by relevance: words in the title or creator rank highest, followed by how often the term appears in the record. The best matches will be in the first two pages of your results.

Faceted search

Filters let you refine your results by structured data – format type, collection, date ranges and subject – and show the number of results per option. If your result set is large, review the filters to see what format, creators or subjects are represented, and select the ones most relevant to your query. Because filters depend on item metadata, they may not represent every collection item; they're under continuous review to become more complete. Once you select a filter, it stays in effect until removed, including if you start a new search.

Refining results

Active filters appear as chips above the results – remove a chip to drop it, or choose Clear all to start again. Sort by relevance, date or title, and switch between grid and list views.

Collection. Limit results to the holdings of a specific collection. New collections and items are increasingly being made available.

Production date. A histogram shows the distribution of results by year, decade or century depending on the range. Use the slider, or enter a start and end year and click Apply.

Creator. Artists, authors, collectors, designers, manufacturers and other contributors. Disambiguate a creator's name from among many results by selecting from the list – full name or birth/death dates – to see only the one(s) you want.

Accession number. Shows how results are distributed across major disciplines. Most commonly a prefix acronym for the collection followed by the year of acquisition.

Object type. The category or physical format of the item – for example book, photograph, musical instrument, scientific equipment or specimen.

Language. Results created in or translated to a specific language.

Digital asset format type. The digital format of works in your results – for example image, PDF, audio or video.

Access. Limit to works available online with a digital asset, or physically 'By appointment' or 'On display'.

Automatic stemming

Your search automatically includes plural and singular forms of your terms, as well as common suffix and tense variations. For example, 'broad' will match 'broader' or 'broadest', but not 'broadly'. Stemming applies within phrase searches as well as keyword searches.

Truncation and wildcards

Use an asterisk to truncate terms that aren't handled by stemming – for example, non-English variations: créat* finds création(s), créateur(s), créatrice(s), etc. You can also truncate from the beginning: *science finds science, conscience, geoscience, omniscience. Use a question mark (?) to find variations of a single letter – kvet?h finds kvetch or kvetsh.

Exact phrase

Use double quotation marks around your terms to match words in the exact order you typed them – for example, "architectural plan".

Advanced search

Want to search a single field – title, creator, subject or date range? Or combine several fields using Contains all (AND), Contains any (OR) or Does not contain (NOT)? Use advanced search when you need to combine two or more specific fields, combine two or more values of a filter, or search items across two selected collections. Results will not include the term if it appears only in a field you haven't selected – basic search returns results across all fields, but the item you're looking for may not be ranked highly.

Boolean

Create complex logic using AND, OR and NOT (all in upper case) and parentheses for nesting.

Indigenous Cultural Data and Access

The University of Melbourne acknowledge Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people as the Traditional Owners of the unceded lands on which we work, learn and live. We pay respect to Elders past, present and future, and acknowledge the importance of Indigenous knowledge in the Academy.

Read about our Indigenous prioritiesRead our full Acknowledgement of Country

Note on images and voices

Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people should be aware that the University of Melbourne website and collections contain the names, images and voices of people who have died.

Note on language

Please be advised that people may be disturbed by the content of some collection items, which may include distressing images and descriptions, and derogatory terms used in their historical context, including for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people. They reflect the period in which they were created and are not the views of the University of Melbourne.

Note on terminology

The following terms are used interchangeably throughout collection items: Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander, First Nations, First Peoples, Indigenous Australians (inclusive of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples), Aboriginal peoples and Indigenous peoples (commonly used for First Nations peoples internationally).

Contact

If you have questions about Indigenous cultural materials, cultural protocols or access conditions, or wish to provide feedback about a record, please contact the relevant collection team.

Copyright and terms of use

How a collection item can be used depends on its copyright licence, intellectual property or cultural advice. Check the licence type and terms of use on each record.

  1. Free access. Items available to download may be reused for personal, research, educational or non-commercial purposes. Please attribute the creator and acknowledge the University of Melbourne.
  2. Licensed. Some items are available under Creative Commons or other licences. Follow the licence terms shown on the record.
  3. Cultural conditions. Where items carry a cultural or advisory notice, engage respectfully with the content. See Indigenous Cultural Data and Access.
  4. Contact the collection when copyright terms are unclear, or to seek permission for publication, online or commercial use. Include the record's persistent link and a description of your intended use.
  5. University Copyright Office. Learn about basic principles of copyright, publishing agreements, licences and contracts, protecting your work, and using copyright material in research.

Access and information

To request access or further information about an item, or to use an image, audio or video, contact the relevant collection and include the record's persistent link. We aim to respond within 5 business days; complex requests may take longer. We also welcome feedback on how to improve the site.

Privacy

The University respects the privacy of anyone who interacts with us and is committed to protecting and managing personal and health information fairly and lawfully. The University is governed by the Privacy and Data Protection Act 2014 (Vic) and the Health Records Act 2000 (Vic), and is a data controller under the EU General Data Protection Regulation for personal information collected from individuals in the EU.
