Search Tips

Here are some tips to help you to easily find collection items.

Basic Search

The search box searches everything. This is the best place to start.

Start with a basic keyword search using two or three keywords. Search failures can result from over-specifying the search.

Things to try when you're getting irrelevant or too many results

  • Add more words.
  • Use the dropdown when a common word is pulling in results you don't want.

The dropdown beside the search box lets you restrict your search to a single part of the record:

Dropdown optionSearches All FieldsThe whole record TitleTitle only CreatorCreator names only SubjectSubject headings only

For example, searching `Melbourne` in All Fields returns anything mentioning Melbourne anywhere; searching it under Title returns only items with Melbourne in the title.

Suggestions appear as you type. These are drawn from terms that exist in the catalogue, so picking one guarantees you'll get results.

Type any combination of title, creator, date, place, or subject terms. All your keywords will appear in a record. This is an AND search.

The best matches will be in the first two pages of the search results.

Results are ranked by their relevance to your search terms, based on:

  • where the words appear in the record (words in the title, or creator are ranked the highest)
  • number of occurrences of the term within the record

Faceted Search

The filter terms in Faceted Search allow you to refine your search results by presenting structured and extended data (format type, collection, date ranges, and subject) and the number of results per field grouping (images – 2,000). If your result set is large, review the filters to see what Format, Creators, or Subjects are represented. Select appropriate filters to view only the most relevant to your query.

Because filters depend on the item metadata, they might not be a complete representation of all the collection items available. These are under continuous review and update to provide the more powerful filters.

Once you select a field limit, it remains in effect until you remove it. That is, if you enter a new search using the Basic Search box the results will be limited by the selected values.

Refining results

Active filters appear as chips above the results. Remove a chip to drop that filter or choose Clear all to start again. Sort by relevance, date or title, and switch between grid and list views.

Collection. Limit the list of results to include only the holdings of a specific collection. New collections and items are increasingly being made available.

Date. A histogram shows the distribution of your results by year, decade, or century, depending on the size of the range. To narrow the date range of your results, use the slider, or enter a start and end year, and click Apply.

Creator. Artists, Authors, Collectors, Designers, Manufacturers, and contributors to works in your results. Disambiguating a creator's name from hundreds of results by using the filter and select from among the list - full name or birth/death dates - to only see the one/s you want.

Organization (as Creator). Corporate or government entities that are identified as creator or are otherwise responsible for content.

Accession Number. Shows how your results are distributed across major disciplines. There are several standards used. Most common is uses a prefix acronym of the collection followed by the year of acquisition.

Object type. This is the category or physical format of the collection item. Use the search option to find all results for a particular type - for example, book, photograph, musical instrument, scientific equipment, or specimen.

Language. Select results created in or translated to a specific language.

Format. The digital format of works in your list of results – for example, image, pdf, audio, and video.

Access. Limit by works available online with a "Digital Asset", or physically 'On Access' or "On Display".

Automatic stemming

Your search automatically includes plural and singular forms of your search terms, as well as common suffix and tense variations.

For example: 'broad' will match records with the term 'broader' or 'broadest' but not 'broadly'

Stemming applies within phrase searches as well as keyword searches.

Truncation and wildcards

Use an asterisk to truncate terms that aren't handled by stemming.

For example, non-English variations - créat* will find création(s), créateur(s), créatrice(s), etc.

You can truncate from the beginning as well - *science finds science, conscience, geoscience, omniscience.

Use a question mark [?] to find variations of a single letter in a word - kvet?h will find kvetch or kvetsh.

Exact phrase

Use double quotation marks around your terms to match words in the exact order you typed them. For example, "architectural plan".

Advanced Search

Want to search a single field — Title, Creator, Subject or Date range?

Or to combine several fields use Contains all (AND), Contains any (OR) or Does not contain (NOT).

Use advanced search when you need to:

  • combine two or more specific fields
  • combine two or more values of a limit
  • items in two select collections

The results will not include the term if they appear in unselected fields. Basic search will return results across all fields but the items you are looking for may not be ranked highly.

Boolean

You can create complex Boolean logic using AND, OR, and NOT (all in upper-case) and parentheses for nesting.
